# Bounding SEL Rates for Null Results and Other Limited Test Data

- Authors: Raymond Ladbury (Goddard Space Flight Center); Matt Joplin (Goddard Space Flight Center); Jean-Marie Lauenstein (Goddard Space Flight Center)

## Abstract

Bounding rates for SEL in Complementary Metal-OxideSemiconductor (CMOS) parts are needed to ensure system reliability [1, 2]. Unfortunately, not all heavy-ion tests produce sufficient cross section (σ) vs. Linear Energy Transfer (LET) data to bound SEL rates by conventional means, and heavy-ion SEE testing may exceed the resources of some missions. Bounding SEL rates is especially difficult when a test yields a
null result at maximum test LET (LET<sub>T</sub>) with fluence (F(LET<sub>T</sub>)) and cannot constrain SEL σ for LET> LET<sub>T</sub>. Constraints from proton testing are even more problematic.[3-8] However,
recent studies have imposed constraints on SEL behavior based on large, diverse historical databases of SEL test data.[9-12] The techniques make the assumption (validated in [12]) that parts in the historical SEL database are representative of SEL susceptible parts generally (and so, of the Device Under Test (DUT) as well). Combining historical data with limited DUT specific test data (e.g. <i>LET<sub>0</sub></i> or <i>σ<sub>s</sub></i> measurements) allows the DUT SEL rate to be bounded. However, this raises the question of how the finite sample in the database can be considered representative of SEL in CMOS parts generally. We propose that the distributions of σ vs. LET fit parameters for in the database can be viewed as a simple example of a coarse-grained representation of the corresponding distributions for SEL susceptible parts generally. Coarse graining “smooths over” fine details of a problem to develop a model that is tractable and still sufficient for predicting system behavior [13]. While SEE data analysis usually must estimate Weibull fit parameters to σ vs. LET, what we really care about are sufficiently accurate SEL rate bounds to assess system reliability impact. If the coarse-grained system produces reasonable rate estimates within some tolerance, it may suffice for part and system reliability studies. We develop techniques to assess the quality of the coarse-grained model and illustrate its utility for bounding SEL rates for null results and other limited data.
